Patterns behind GP appointments

Who misses appointments, does booking further ahead matter, and when are slots wasted? Explore 110,000+ appointments from a GP practice yourself.

The data is a mock dataset, so no real patients are shown. The charts load the full dataset in your browser, so they can take a few seconds to appear.

Question 1

Who misses appointments?

Each bar shows the mix of appointment outcomes for one group, scaled to 100% so groups of different sizes can be compared fairly.

Click a bar to filter the other two charts to that group. Shift-click to pick more than one. Use the boxes to hide outcomes. Hover for exact figures.

Loading 110,000 appointments…
  • Attendance is strikingly consistent: about 75–80% of appointments are attended in every age group, for both sexes and across insurers.
  • Differences appear only in small subgroups. Among patients aged 90+, 15% on HelloSpring Vital cancelled, compared with 23% on CuraQuark Alliance.
Question 2

Does booking further ahead matter?

Outcomes, waiting time and cancellation rate by the number of days between booking and the appointment, over a 30-day window.

Hover over the top-left chart to draw a matching line across all three charts.

Loading…
  • The share of attended appointments stays stable whatever the booking interval.
  • Mean waiting time holds at about 44–45 minutes, rising to nearly 50 minutes at 29–30 days.
  • Cancellation rates move between about 12% and 18% with no clear trend.
Question 3

When are slots wasted?

Day of the week against time of day. The left heatmap shows the share of missed appointments; the right shows the share of slots occupied.

Hover over a cell for the exact rate and the number of appointments or slots behind it.

  • Friday 10–11am and Tuesday 4–5pm have the most missed appointments; Wednesday 11am–12pm the fewest.
  • Friday 11am–12pm has the highest occupancy, at about 91%.
  • The ranges are narrow (21–23% missed, 88–91% occupied), so colour differences are small in absolute terms.
